# Good Samaritan Shelter
## Making Community Support Easier to Find

**Role:** Product Management Co-lead  
**Team:** 6-person cross-functional volunteer team  
**Organization:** Develop for Good × Good Samaritan Shelter  
**Focus:** Product Discovery · UX Research · Requirements · Prioritization · Cross-functional Delivery

### Project Overview

Good Samaritan Shelter (GSS) is a California Central Coast nonprofit offering services including emergency shelter, recovery support, housing assistance, and job training.

Our team worked with GSS to redesign its website and make critical service information easier to find, while also improving how the organization communicates its mission and impact to donors and community partners.

As a Product Management Co-lead, I worked on stakeholder communication, user research, product requirements, scope prioritization, and coordination with the design and engineering team.

### 1. The Problem

The existing website did not fully reflect the range of services offered by GSS. Service information was difficult to discover, there was no centralized searchable directory, and updating website content was not straightforward for staff.

We needed to consider several distinct user groups:

- People seeking emergency or long-term support
- Social workers searching for resources on behalf of clients
- Donors and community partners learning about the organization's impact

The product challenge was to make the website easier to navigate for people seeking immediate help while supporting the nonprofit's broader communication and operational needs.

### 2. Understanding User Needs

We conducted user research through a survey that received 67 responses, with varying response counts across individual questions.

The findings helped us understand what users needed most when searching for community support.

**Key research findings**

- 54 of 61 respondents selected address and location information as something they wanted to find quickly.
- 47 of 61 selected phone numbers or email addresses.
- 40 of 61 selected operating hours.
- 42 of 59 respondents explicitly selected smartphones as the device they most frequently used to search for help.

These findings reinforced the importance of accessible, clearly organized service information and mobile-friendly navigation.

The research also highlighted a separate set of donor needs, including straightforward donation options, impact stories, and clear explanations of programs.

### 3. Translating Research into Product Requirements

Based on stakeholder requirements and research findings, our team defined several priority areas:

**Service discovery:** Provide a searchable directory that helps visitors find relevant services by keyword, category, and location.

**Navigation:** Make key actions such as getting help, searching services, and donating easy to discover.

**Mobile accessibility:** Support users accessing information from smartphones.

**Content management:** Help GSS staff update service listings, news, and other website content without requiring extensive engineering support.

**Consistent experience:** Maintain coherent branding, navigation, and presentation across different programs and services.

I contributed to the Product Requirements Document, including user stories and acceptance criteria that helped translate business and user needs into actionable development requirements.

### 4. Defining Scope and Making Tradeoffs

One important part of the project was balancing user needs with the team's delivery constraints.

The PRD explored a React-based frontend, reusable UI components, Fuse.js for service search, and Retool for administrative content management.

These choices reflected considerations around implementation effort, ongoing maintenance, cost, and usability for nonprofit staff.

The scope also distinguished core website improvements from separate initiatives that could be deferred, helping the team focus on delivering a coherent experience.

### 5. From Requirements to Wireframes

The wireframes translated the product direction into concrete user experiences.

The homepage design emphasized prominent actions such as **Get Help Now**, **Search Services**, and **Donate**, together with direct access to major service categories.

These elements connected to two central research themes: helping service seekers find actionable information quickly and helping donors understand and support GSS's work.

### 6. Cross-functional Delivery

Working with designers, engineers, and nonprofit stakeholders, I helped turn research findings and project goals into documented requirements and prioritized scope.

The project involved stakeholder feedback, usability testing, user stories, acceptance criteria, and coordination across product, design, and engineering responsibilities.

The redesigned website was ultimately launched and remains in use by Good Samaritan Shelter.

### 7. Outcome & Reflection

The project delivered a functioning website redesign and created a more structured foundation for presenting services and organizational information.

We also established a documented set of product requirements, user flows, and implementation considerations to guide development.

I do not have verified post-launch conversion or engagement metrics, so I distinguish the successful launch from measurable downstream outcomes.

**What I learned**

This project reinforced that product management is not simply deciding which features to build. It requires understanding different users, identifying the most important problems, translating findings into clear requirements, and making practical decisions under constraints.

It also showed me how valuable a shared Product Requirements Document can be when product, design, engineering, and business stakeholders need to work toward the same outcome.
